Riemann Hypothesis solved?

Three days ago, on November 20th, 2026, Levent Alpöge1 claimed to have solved2 the Riemann Hypothesis3. The next day, the LEAN4 community verified5 the proof, cementing Alpöge as one of the most significant and controversial mathematicians of our decade, hell maybe even century.

There are some issues with the existence of not only Alpöge’s RH solution, but for all of the others in the future of AI-assisted proofs. As of now, no person or group of mathematicians have been able to dissect and analyze the proof; it’s only been confirmed with LEAN. What’s the point of a proof if nobody understands it or is able to follow its direction? Ingenuity and discovery across centuries are found in the heart of the process. Hindrance is almost certain if no one meticulously runs their fingers along every groove and bump in this proof.

As an aspiring mathematician and polymath, I find this on one hand, exciting, and on the other hand, foreboding. It’s remarkable that with the help of AI, a proof, albeit supposedly over 250,000 pages and seventeen million lines of LEAN long, was able to solve a problem that’s puzzled generations of mathematicians. However, the development of Alpöge’s solution marks the start of a precarious era for mathematics.

Far greater than just the ability to proceed forward as mathematicians, the formalization of RH’s proof brings up the possibility that the once romantic approach to mathematics as a philosophy and art may be over. Just as the old industrial revolution ended the role of the craftsman and artisan, this new era of superintelligence poses a threat to the existence of mathematics wholly.

Modern, capitalist, society forces individuals to survive off a single cadence only: make money or die. Afraid of death, the worker brutally battles for any position to devote and enslave themselves to a greater machine with no shred of sympathy for them. Mathematicians, philosophers, artists, and scientists have long been at the back of the line, seemingly invincible from capitalism’s venomous touch. However, their death is inevitable. RH’s solution, as profound as it is, has contributed to the expedition of the death of art and thus, humanity.

I don’t mean to be sentimental or an existentialist but the facts remain. On November 20th, 2026, hundreds of PhDs died. Hundreds of future mathematicians, philosophers, and visionaries died. People who’ve devoted their life and mind towards humanity and understanding have been stomped by a creature that doesn’t even know what it means to be human.

Just months ago, at this summer program I went to for math, me and my friends would make jokes about the problem.
“RH will be proved before you talk to a girl”
“We’ll get a proof for RH before GTA VI.”
Jokes of that nature. Now, unfortunately, those silly jabs have been realized. It’s like watching your world being burned down right before your eyes.

“People’s dreams… have no end!”
- Marshall D. Teach (Blackbeard) - One Piece6, Eiichiro Oda

However silly, Blackbeard’s speech from One Piece reminds us that a light exists even in a dark fog like this. His remark comes from a moment where an entire town mocks and beats the protagonist of the anime, Monkey D. Luffy, for believing in the great treasure, the One Piece. Although a later antagonist, Blackbeard, after witnessing Luffy’s assault, exclaims that the townspeople, simply put, are full of shit. Excuse my language, but there’s no better way to get this across. The romantic era of piracy, will never be over so long as people can dream beyond the stretch of imagination.

We have to remind ourselves too, that our ability to create and explore is limited solely by our own minds. The world can tell us that our research is obsolete, that we serve no purpose. Many may be laid off in the process. Regardless of the challenges, we’ll never be stripped of our ingenuity and creativity. I may die homeless, stripped, barren, but I’ll have been a mathematician to the very end.
